Software
________

`Population genetics simulations <pop_lab/index.html>`_ are the simulations
written for our population genetics practices. Each one is a small interactive
application: set the parameters of a population, run it, and watch the allele
frequencies respond.

`Sequence comparison tools <edu/software/alignment/index.html>`_ teach basic
bioinformatic concepts: the `dotplot <edu/software/alignment/#dotplot>`_,
`inversions <edu/software/alignment/#inversion>`_,
`manual alignment <edu/software/alignment/#manual>`_, which scores an alignment
typed by hand, `Smith-Waterman <edu/software/alignment/#alignment>`_, and
`BLAST words <edu/software/alignment/#blast>`_, which turn a database search
into a lookup.

`SNP calling <edu/software/snp_calling/index.html>`_ is the practice for
statistical inference: one simulated set of reads called three times over --
with thresholds, by `maximum likelihood <edu/software/snp_calling/#likelihood>`_
and by `Bayes <edu/software/snp_calling/#bayes>`_ -- and scored against a truth
that is known because it was simulated.

`Do cows fly? <edu/software/cow_p_val/index.html>`_ is the p-value practice. A
friend claims that cows fly, for so many hours a day. Every visit to every farm
finds every cow on the ground, so what changes from one experiment to the next
is the farm -- and a field of nine cows with nothing in the air is the same
observation as a field of ninety, and nothing like the same evidence. At one
hour a day it takes a farm of 71 cows before that empty sky rejects the claim.

`A positive HIV test <edu/software/infection_prob/index.html>`_ is the inverse
probability practice. The doctor can quote the test's false positive rate,
P(positive | not infected); what the person wants is P(infected | positive). The
page works both out from the prevalence and the error rates of the test, and
shows the same answer again as whole people.

`The likelihood of a coin <edu/software/likelihood_coin/index.html>`_ is the
maximum likelihood practice, done by hand. The
`first tab <edu/software/likelihood_coin/#value>`_ works out the likelihood of
one P(heads) -- the probability of the count of heads seen, if the coin landed
heads that often -- and the `second <edu/software/likelihood_coin/#explore>`_
moves P(heads) with a slider, leaving a trail, to go looking for the value that
makes the tosses likeliest.

`A coin, three ways <edu/software/bayesian_coin/index.html>`_ takes one string
of coin tosses and reasons about it three times over: a
`classical test <edu/software/bayesian_coin/#test>`_,
`maximum likelihood <edu/software/bayesian_coin/#mle>`_ and
`Bayes <edu/software/bayesian_coin/#bayesian>`_, with all three answers on show
at once. Ten heads out of ten reject a fair coin, estimate the bias as exactly
one with an interval of zero width, and leave a posterior mean of 0.917: the same
tosses, three languages. Toss by toss, one at a time, so how much evidence it
takes to move each of them can be seen rather than asserted.

All of them run entirely in the browser: there is nothing to install, and no
sequence you type is sent anywhere.
